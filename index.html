The loop

Four steps, in this order. It is a cycle: step IV takes you back to I.

I

Recruit

You open a bag at the tavern and an outlaw comes out. Paid in $BOUNTY: 90% goes to the vault that pays everyone, 10% to the treasury.

II

Rob

Send them to work. On the map they break crates, find chests and dodge traps; the vault pays every epoch, split by weight.

III

Repair

Every epoch of work spends useful life. At zero the outlaw is captured, and low life also lowers the fusion odds. Repairing costs $BOUNTY, which goes back to the vault.

IV

Fuse

Two become one of a higher rank — if the odds allow. The odds are shown before you confirm, always.

Never two alike

This is not a slogan. It is a sum you can check.

561,254,400

visually distinct looks — combining rank, body build, five head types, four eye shapes, four tunics, seven pieces of gear, beard, scarf, skin, hair and the cloth's hue shift. Adding the three stats, which roll with two decimal places, the space passes 6.2 quintillion combinations.

With 10,000 recruits, the chance of two identical ones stays below 0.000001%. Every outlaw is born from a 32-byte seed: the same seed always returns the same outlaw, with the same stats and the same pixels. The traits are derived with sha256 on purpose — it runs exactly the same in Node and inside a Solidity contract, so the chain can prove what the generator produced.

The economy

P2E games usually die the same way: fixed emission drains the vault, the vault hits zero, the game ends in a single day. These four decisions exist so that doesn't happen — and none of them is a kindness, they are all math.

Variable emission

The vault never pays a fixed amount per head. Every epoch it pays a fraction of itself (0.5%), split by weight.

Consequence: the vault decays asymptotically and never reaches zero. If inflow dries up, the reward shrinks smoothly — the game gets smaller instead of dying.

Rarity competes for a share, it doesn't drain the vault

A Legend earns 8× what a Nobody earns. But the total paid per epoch is the same, whether the gang has one Legend or a hundred.

Rarity redistributes between players; it doesn't increase the drain. That keeps the fun of hunting ranks without the top emptying the common vault.

Useful life measured in work

Life is spent per epoch worked, not per calendar day. Whoever stops playing is not punished: gone for three months, you come back and find everything as you left it.

And the bot farm that runs 24 hours a day spends life twice as fast as someone who plays half the time — so it pays twice the repairs. The bot becomes a customer instead of a leak.

Captured, not burned

Zero useful life does not destroy the NFT. The outlaw is captured: stops working, leaves the split, but stays in your wallet, with its art, and transferable. It comes out by paying a ransom.

For the math it is the same — whoever is locked up doesn't share the vault. The difference is that nobody wakes up to an NFT erased by the contract.

per_head = (r x Vault) x weight / sum(weights)

sum of all   = r x Vault        always, whatever the gang
steady state: Vault* = inflow / r

An example with real-money numbers, so nobody fools themselves: with r = 0.5% per epoch, a vault worth $100k and a thousand active outlaws, each one earns $0.50 per epoch. A bag at $50 pays for itself in 100 epochs — and only if the vault stays at $100k, which requires selling, in bags, as much as it pays out: 0.5% of the vault every epoch, forever.

What doesn't exist yet, and what can go wrong

  • The contracts are on testnet only, with open code on the explorer and 63 automated tests, but no external audit. The token here is worth nothing.
  • It is not a skill game: the map shows the gang working, but how much each one earns is decided by the contract (the outlaw's weight × the vault).
  • The economy is sustainable in tokens, but real-money value depends on the average player spending more than they cash out. That is game-design work, not tokenomics, and it is where most P2E games fail.
  • A paid bag with a random result is a loot box. The odds are shown before every confirmation by design — but the topic is under regulatory discussion in several countries, including Brazil.
  • Nothing here is a promise of financial return.
  • Play with a test-only wallet. OUTLAWS never asks for your secret phrase, never messages you offering support, and never asks for an "unlimited" token approval. The official address is the one on this page.
